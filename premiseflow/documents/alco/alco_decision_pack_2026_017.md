## Paper ALCO-2026-017 - Liquidity buffer strategy

### Recommendation

Maintain the current liquidity buffer strategy and hold HQLA at the existing
level. Do not pre-fund additional term wholesale capacity in the coming two
quarters.

### Supporting analysis

The Liquidity Stress Model projects an LCR of **118%** at the reporting date,
against an internal management threshold of 110% and a regulatory minimum of
100%. Headroom to the internal threshold is therefore 8 percentage points.

The projection applies a stable retail deposit runoff rate of 5%. That runoff
rate is calibrated directly to the approved retail deposit behaviour assumption
**A-DEP-001 version 1**, which holds that retail salary-account deposits retain
at least 90% of incumbent balances over a rolling ninety-day horizon under normal
operating conditions.

The Committee noted that total deposits have been stable to modestly growing and
that no aggregate funding stress indicator is currently elevated.

### Sensitivity disclosed to the Committee

Treasury Risk disclosed that the recommendation is sensitive to the retail
retention premise. Each percentage point of retention shortfall below the
approved 90% premise increases the applicable stable runoff rate by
approximately 0.8 percentage points, which reduces the projected LCR. A
sustained shortfall of the order of ten percentage points would be expected to
eliminate the headroom to the internal threshold entirely.

The Committee accepted this sensitivity on the basis that A-DEP-001 had been
independently validated (MV-2025-042) and that no evidence of deterioration was
present at the time of the meeting.

### Decision

**Approved as recommended.** Rationale recorded: modelled LCR remains comfortably
above the 110% internal threshold under the approved retail deposit behaviour
assumptions, including retail salary deposit retention of 90% over a rolling
ninety-day horizon (A-DEP-001 v1).

### Dependency declaration

| Assumption | Version | Reliance |
|---|---|---|
| A-DEP-001 Retail Salary Deposit Stability | v1 | **Material** |
| A-DEP-002 Deposit Beta Ceiling | v1 | Supporting |

### Review trigger

This decision is to be brought back to the Committee if the retail deposit
retention assumption is challenged or breached, or if the LCR projection falls
below the internal threshold on any basis.

## Paper ALCO-2026-011 - Retail funds transfer pricing curve refresh

### Recommendation

Adopt the refreshed retail FTP curve with a stable-deposit credit of 38 basis
points for payroll-linked balances.

### Supporting analysis

The stable-deposit credit compensates the retail franchise for supplying
behaviourally stable funding. Its size is calibrated to the behavioural life of
the balances, which follows from the approved retention premise. A 90% ninety-day
retention premise supports a materially longer behavioural life, and therefore a
higher credit, than a lower premise would.

### Decision

**Approved as recommended.**

### Dependency declaration

| Assumption | Version | Reliance |
|---|---|---|
| A-DEP-001 Retail Salary Deposit Stability | v1 | **Material** |
| A-DEP-002 Deposit Beta Ceiling | v1 | Supporting |

## Paper ALCO-2026-009 - Mortgage prepayment model recalibration cadence

### Recommendation

Move mortgage prepayment model recalibration from annual to semi-annual cadence.

### Supporting analysis

Observed conditional prepayment rates have shown increased dispersion within the
approved 6% to 11% band. More frequent recalibration reduces model error without
changing the approved assumption.

### Decision

**Approved as recommended.**

|---|---|---|
