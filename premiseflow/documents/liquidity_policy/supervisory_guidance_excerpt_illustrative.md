## 1. Behavioural assumptions in prudential calculations

Firms using behavioural assumptions in prudential liquidity calculations would
ordinarily be expected to demonstrate that each assumption is documented,
evidenced, owned, approved, and monitored against realised outcomes.

## 2. Ongoing validity

An assumption evidenced under one set of market and commercial conditions does
not automatically remain valid when those conditions change. Firms would
ordinarily be expected to record the conditions under which an assumption was
evidenced, and to treat a change in those conditions as a trigger for
re-evidencing, independently of whether the headline metric has moved.

## 3. Aggregation and masking

Where a behavioural assumption concerns the stability of an identified cohort,
monitoring performed on an aggregate population may fail to detect
deterioration in that cohort. This is a recognised weakness where the firm is
concurrently acquiring new balances, since new inflows can offset and conceal
attrition from the existing population. Monitoring would ordinarily be expected
to be performed at the level at which the assumption is stated.

## 4. Consequential review of prior decisions

Where an assumption supporting a prudential metric is found no longer to hold,
firms would ordinarily be expected to identify the decisions previously taken in
reliance on that assumption and to consider whether those decisions remain
appropriate. Records should preserve what was believed, what evidence existed,
and what was decided at the time, without retrospective alteration.

## 5. Use of automated tooling

Automated analytics may be used to detect and evidence assumption deterioration.
Accountability for confirming that an assumption no longer holds, and for
approving any revised assumption, would ordinarily be expected to rest with an
identified accountable individual rather than with an automated process.
